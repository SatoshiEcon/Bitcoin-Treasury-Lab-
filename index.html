Step 3: The metrics

What's happening: these are the metrics Strategy reports. mNAV is market cap divided by the market value of the bitcoin. Above 1, investors pay a premium for each dollar of bitcoin the company holds, which lets it issue stock to buy more. Bitcoin yield is the percent change in bitcoin per diluted share. It is a company KPI, not a yield in the bond sense, and it ignores debt and preferred claims.

Step 4: Scenario test (down and up)

What's happening: bitcoin has fallen more than 50% several times, and it has also more than doubled in a single year. A CFO shows the board both. On the downside, the question is whether the company can still cover its debt, interest and preferred dividends. On the upside, fair-value accounting puts the gain straight into reported earnings. That swings net income, can create tax and covenant questions, and tends to push the mNAV premium up, which tempts management to issue more stock and buy more bitcoin.
